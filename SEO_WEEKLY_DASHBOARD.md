## 3. Query Discovery System (Mining New GSC Queries)

Do not rely solely on predefined keywords. In GSC **Performance** -> **Search Results**:
1. Filter by: **Date:** *Last 28 days*.
2. Sort queries by **Impressions** (Descending).
3. Identify long-tail search queries with:
   - High impressions (> 100)
   - Position between 8 and 25
   - Low CTR (< 2%)
4. Add newly discovered queries to the editorial roadmap or enhance existing guides.

---

## 4. Low-CTR Opportunity Triage Protocol

When a page has high impressions but low CTR:

```text
Step 1: Check Search Intent
   ├── Is the query informational, navigational, or transactional?
   └── Does the snippet directly answer what the researcher is looking for?
       ↓
Step 2: Review <title> Tag
   ├── Ensure Title Case, under 60 characters
   ├── Place primary intent keyword near the beginning
   └── Include value proposition (e.g. "Step-by-Step Guide", "Word Template Included")
       ↓
Step 3: Review Meta Description
   ├── Ensure 140–160 characters
   ├── Include a clear Direct Answer summary + actionable CTA
   └── Avoid vague marketing language or repetitive keyword lists
       ↓
Step 4: Review Page Intro & Structure
   ├── Ensure the direct answer appears in the first 50 words
   └── Use structured comparison tables and step-by-step headings (H2/H3)
```

> [!CAUTION]
> **ANTI-KEYWORD STUFFING RULE**: Never stuff keywords or alter titles solely for keyword density. All headings and metadata must maintain authentic scholarly tone and academic clarity.
